Why this workshop

Every layer now constrains the others

Building and running AI systems has become an infrastructure problem that no single discipline owns. How fast models train, how cheaply they can be served, and where they can be deployed, if at all, depend on constraints that run from transistors and accelerator architectures through interconnects, memory, and serving stacks, and outward into datacenter thermal design, siting, interconnection queues, and regional grid capacity.

Decisions at each layer increasingly affect the others, yet the people working on them rarely sit in the same room. Universities also have limited visibility into which problems matter most in practice. This workshop brings U-M faculty together with the organizations building and overseeing this infrastructure to hear directly where the hard problems are.

Michigan expertise across the whole span

Michigan has unusual depth across computer architecture and hardware design, systems and networking, machine learning and data management, security and privacy, power systems and control, and the mechanical and civil engineering expertise behind thermal management, siting, and the built environment.

For industry participants

This is a chance to shape a new research program at Michigan from the start. Bring the problems you are actually stuck on: participants will help decide which challenges U-M takes on and how, working with faculty across every layer of the AI stack, from accelerators and serving systems to cooling, siting, and the grid.

You will also meet the students doing this work.

What comes out of it

A shared agenda and a research program built together

Michigan Report on AI Infrastructure for 2030

A short report on the grand challenges across the stack, from performance and cost to keeping new infrastructure safe and affordable for host communities.
